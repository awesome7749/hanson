# Heat Pumps and Power Outages: Make a Simple Plan Before the Storm

**Draft — unpublished · English · reviewed October 2, 2026**

Choose the essentials, discuss backup power and know where to find Massachusetts safety guidance.

A heat pump needs electricity. If a winter storm interrupts power, the useful question is what your household will do next. Make a small plan on a calm afternoon rather than trying to design one during an outage.

## Quick summary

- List the household essentials before choosing backup power.
- Have backup equipment reviewed for your exact heating system.
- Follow official generator and carbon-monoxide guidance.

## Know what depends on power

Your heat pump’s compressor, fans and controls need electricity. Do not assume a retained furnace or boiler will operate either: many fuel-burning systems also need powered controls, blowers or pumps. Ask your service technician about the actual equipment.

Write down the heating arrangement and contact information in a place everyone can find. Include the utility’s outage-reporting channel.

## Choose your priorities

Massachusetts emergency guidance recommends an emergency kit, working alarms and a family plan. Add your household’s specific needs, including medical equipment, communication and a place to stay if the home cannot be kept safe.

Our practical recommendation is to list essential loads before shopping for backup power. Heating one area, keeping food cold and powering an entire home are different project goals.

Source: [Massachusetts: Power Outage Safety Tips](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/power-outage-safety-tips)

## Review backup power professionally

Ask a licensed electrician and your HVAC technician whether the proposed generator or battery can support the selected equipment and other essential loads. Ask for the expected operating limits and, for batteries, the assumptions behind the runtime estimate.

Massachusetts fire-safety guidance calls for a professionally installed transfer switch when connecting a generator to the home’s electrical system. Operate generators outdoors and follow current placement instructions. Never use an oven or an indoor grill for emergency heat.

Source: [Massachusetts: Generator Safety](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/generator-safety)

Source: [Massachusetts: Carbon Monoxide Safety](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/carbon-monoxide-co-safety)

## Plan the return to normal

Keep the equipment manual accessible and ask the installer how your system restarts after an outage. Check settings when power returns and contact service if the system reports an error or fails to resume normal operation.

Hanson Home recommends including outage questions in the installation handoff. A short written plan is easier for the whole household to use than a collection of guesses.
