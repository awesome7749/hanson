# A Quieter Heat-Pump Installation Starts With the Outdoor Location

**Draft — unpublished · English · reviewed October 2, 2026**

Four questions to ask about windows, mounting and the spaces around the outdoor unit.

You will live with the outdoor unit’s location long after installation day. Before settling on a spot, walk the area with your installer. A short conversation about sound and daily use can help you choose a location you are comfortable with.

## Quick summary

- Discuss bedrooms, patios and neighboring spaces.
- Ask about sound data for the actual model.
- Review mounting and access as part of the same plan.

## Look beyond the easiest wall

Point out your bedroom windows, favorite outdoor seating and nearby neighbors’ windows. Ask the installer to explain the proposed position in relation to those spaces.

At Hanson Home, we recommend reviewing the site from both inside and outside. A convenient pipe route is one consideration; how you use the house should also be part of the discussion.

## Ask what the sound figure means

If a proposal mentions decibels, ask whether the number is sound power or sound pressure and under what operating conditions it was measured. Compare like-for-like information for the exact models.

Ask whether operation changes between mild weather and a demanding winter day. Avoid treating one advertised “quiet” number as a promise of what you will hear at every window.

## Review the mounting choice

PNNL’s installation guidance identifies noise criteria as a design consideration and notes potential vibration issues when outdoor equipment is attached to a wall. Ask how the mounting method suits the proposed position.

Also ask how required clearances, snow conditions, drainage and service access will be maintained. Do not add a decorative enclosure without the installer checking it against the equipment instructions.

## Finish with a walk-through

Before approving the layout, stand at the proposed spot and ask: can it be serviced, where does water go, and which nearby spaces may hear it? Confirm any local siting or noise requirements with the installer.

Request that the agreed location and mounting approach appear in the written scope. If an unusual rattle or vibration appears after installation, report it for a service check rather than assuming it is normal.
