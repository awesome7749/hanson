# Before Your Heat-Pump Quote: Make the Most of a Mass Save Assessment

**Draft — unpublished · English · reviewed October 2, 2026**

A short preparation checklist that helps turn an energy assessment into a useful plan for your home.

Thinking about a heat pump? Start with a clear picture of the house. A Mass Save Home Energy Assessment can help you identify improvements; a heat-pump proposal explains the equipment and installation. Bringing the two conversations together makes the next step easier.

## Quick summary

- Confirm your household’s program eligibility.
- Bring comfort concerns and questions about the house.
- Share the assessment report with your installer.

## Know what the visit is for

Mass Save describes its assessment as a review of energy-saving opportunities, followed by an energy report with recommendations and available incentives. Confirm eligibility and appointment details through the program for your address.

Use the visit to understand the house as a whole. A room that feels uncomfortable, an attic you rarely visit and equipment you hope to replace are all useful conversation starters.

Source: [Mass Save: Homeowners Get Started](https://www.masssave.com/get-started/homeowners)

## Prepare a five-minute home brief

Write down where you feel drafts, which rooms you avoid in winter and what you would like to improve in summer. Add the age of your current heating equipment if you know it. You do not need a perfect building history.

Have any previous assessment report, recent utility bills and planned renovations handy. Our recommendation: choose your three most important questions before the visit so they do not get lost in the discussion.

## Turn the report into a sequence

Afterward, ask which recommendations should come first and which need a separate contractor’s review. Put the report somewhere you can find it again rather than treating it as a finished project.

If you expect to add insulation or air sealing, tell the HVAC installer what is planned. Ask whether those changes affect the heating design and when the equipment selection should be finalized.

## Bring the house plan to the equipment conversation

At Hanson Home, we recommend sharing the report when requesting a heat-pump proposal. Ask the installer to explain the rooms served, equipment location and any unresolved work.

Make a small next-step list: who you will contact, what information they need and which decision comes next. The goal is a coordinated plan you understand before you book installation.
